# Application / workspace adequacy

Use when people create, inspect, organize, or act on persistent work.

## Jobs and connected expectations

- Orient people to place, objects, current state, and available actions.
- Keep the primary task and its supporting context visible at the right density.
- Support create, inspect, edit, save, undo, recover, and return as the task needs.
- Make selection, permissions, consequences, and feedback unambiguous.
- Preserve efficiency for repeated/expert use without making first use opaque.

Choose a structure such as list/detail, canvas with inspectors, queue and record,
multi-pane workspace, or focused sequence from actual object relationships. A
single representative screen may be enough for a visual direction request. A flow
or product concept needs connected states that prove its core operation.

Common shallow failures: dashboard cards replacing the real workspace; navigation
without an object model; actions detached from their selection scope; happy-path
fixtures with no save/error/empty behavior; excessive onboarding compensating for
unclear structure; visual novelty lowering scan speed.

Enough means the user can explain what exists, what is selected, what can happen,
what changed, and how to recover within the requested scope. Do not invent account,
collaboration, analytics, settings, or admin surfaces unless the task implies them.
