# Marketing / product / persuasion adequacy

Use when the requested artifact must earn understanding, trust, and action.

## Jobs and connected expectations

- Make the proposition and intended audience legible early.
- Show how the product, service, or idea changes the user's situation.
- Supply credible proof or mechanism appropriate to the stakes.
- Resolve consequential objections and make the next action clear.
- Preserve orientation and trust through pricing, signup, demo, or contact when
  those connections are part of the request.

Possible structures include problem-to-outcome narrative, product demonstration,
evidence-led comparison, editorial argument, or direct offer. Choose the progression
that fits the content. A homepage request usually needs more than an isolated hero;
a hero request does not authorize inventing the rest of the funnel.

Common shallow failures: slogan plus feature cards; repeated claims without proof;
screenshots with no explanatory role; every section competing at equal intensity;
generic logos or testimonials presented as facts; a primary action detached from
the decision it asks people to make.

Enough means the requested unit supports the intended decision with a coherent
content progression and honest next action. Use realistic placeholder copy for a
concept, but label unverifiable names, statistics, reviews, and customer claims.
