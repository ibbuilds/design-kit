# Portfolio / showcase adequacy

Use when the requested artifact presents a person's or studio's body of work.

## Jobs and connected expectations

- Establish identity, positioning, point of view, and the kind of work offered.
- Let a visitor recognize and select relevant work without decoding decoration.
- Give selected work enough context, role, process/evidence, and outcome to judge it.
- Communicate background or capabilities when they affect fit and credibility.
- Offer a clear, credible path to contact, availability, or another intended action.

The architecture may be an index with project detail, a continuous narrative, a
filtered collection, or another coherent model. Choose from content and audience;
do not mechanically create About, Work, Services, and Contact sections. For a full
portfolio experience, verify the transition from identity to browsing to project
depth and next action. For a project page request, judge only the project story and
its necessary navigation context.

Common shallow failures: a hero and thumbnail grid standing in for the experience;
projects distinguished only by imagery; detail pages with no role, constraints, or
outcomes; biography disconnected from work; decorative transitions that obscure
selection; contact hidden behind aesthetic restraint.

Enough depends on scope. A hero request is enough when the hero resolves identity,
positioning, and the requested action. A complete concept needs credible overview
and detail behavior plus an intentional path onward. Do not add testimonials,
awards, clients, services, or metrics without real content or clearly marked
placeholders.
