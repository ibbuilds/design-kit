# Asset direction

Load when a specific visual-material opportunity merits investigation or an asset
needs preparation. These are
design heuristics, revised 2026-09-05 after an observed semantic failure, not a
mandatory planning phase. Keep decisions inside the primary session.

## Meaning before generation

Identify the section, compositional job and expected gain over the current native material:
comprehension, identity, atmosphere or a focal relationship. Keep exploration to that
opportunity; reuse an available anchor, investigate only an actual uncertainty, and generate
only when the proposed benefit is credible. One focal asset needs no family workflow.
Prefer omission to a bad asset, but do not treat credible native geometry as proof that
better material is unnecessary. Premium ambition alone does not require imagery.

Before generation, form a compact **asset semantic contract**, in a few clauses:

- **Depiction and purpose:** what exists; why it belongs; what the viewer should
  understand/feel; the exact section content or interaction it supports.
- **Visual mapping:** literal subject or legible metaphor; identifying cues that
  must survive abstraction; intended material/form and scale; likely wrong reading.
- **Placement and failure:** focal/supporting/atmospheric/explanatory/structural role;
  region, aspect/crop, type/UI/whitespace relationship, desktop/mobile treatment;
  what would make it misleading, generic, technically defective or expendable.

This is not a file, user report or schema to fill mechanically. Resolve material
unknowns from the current artifact/brief; do not generate first and find a use later.

## Visual anchor and generation direction

Use permitted evidence via [visual references](../visual-references.md). Query the
specific unresolved mechanism: subject recognition, document construction, readable
camera angle, degree of abstraction, edge extraction, light or type-safe framing.
A reference about lighting does not also prove the subject/metaphor is legible.
Inspect the useful region at sufficient scale; retrieve a complementary example only
where evidence is missing. Metadata and a matching mood are not visual solutions.
Choose a **visual anchor** when an important asset needs a precise visual world: usable
project material, user references or inspected permitted library evidence. Name what it
anchors (subject cues, material, light, camera, palette or atmosphere) and what must not
transfer. A lighting anchor need not dictate composition or identity. References guide
original work; their access does not authorize copying proprietary assets.

Translate the contract into generation instructions with only consequential controls:
subject and identifying cues, physical construction/material, perspective and scale,
focal placement/negative space, light direction/softness, contact and occlusion,
color relationships, realism/simplification, and likely failure exclusions. Specify
the intended crop and surroundings. Lens/texture detail belongs only when it changes
the result. Do not forbid the very cues needed to recognize the subject.

Use the current built-in image-generation/editing capability and its skill when available;
inspect its actual tool contract. Preserve user assets and edit invariants. Do not add a
provider dependency, infer mask/path parameters, or silently switch to a paid/API workflow.
If generation, inspection or extraction is unavailable, state the gap and choose an
authorized native/usable alternative; never claim success. Use meaningful directions,
not automatically longer prompts or repeated independent generations.

## Master and related assets

When several assets must share a world, establish one visually verified master before
deriving crops, angles, isolates, scenes or responsive variants. Keep its exact image
available as an edit/reference input through the supported tool contract; a repeated
text prompt alone is weak continuity control. Reuse a crop when it solves the need.
State only the invariants that matter: subject/identity, geometry, styling, camera,
light direction and temperature, materials, grain or atmosphere; specify what changes.

Compare each result with the master and its eventual neighbors, not only in isolation.
Drifting perspective, unrelated skin treatment or incompatible shadows fail the family
even when each picture looks good. A changed master invalidates earlier family comparisons;
recheck affected members and placements. One isolated asset needs no family machinery.
Keep selected design assets with the user's deliverable/storage context, outside the
reusable package; generated candidates are not automatically reference-corpus entries.

## Reject intention–pixel mismatches

Inspect the actual generated pixels **before placement**, describing what is visibly
depicted before comparing it with the contract. Ask: without knowing the prompt,
would a viewer read the intended object or association? A document metaphor needs
visible document cues; attractive plate-like geometry with no such cues fails even
if its color and translucency match. For intentional abstraction, test its promised
association and compositional job rather than demand a literal object.

Judge separately: semantic recognition, visual quality, technical integrity,
framing/composition, material/light coherence, brand fit, section relevance and final
integration. Semantic or contextual failure is a veto, not something beauty or six
other passes can average away. Do not relabel an unintended result to justify it.

Use the relevant craft standard, not one universal realism test:

| Material | Inspect what can betray the intended language |
|---|---|
| Photographic people | Anatomy, hands/eyes, skin and hair, fabric/pose, gravity/contact, lens and depth consistency, credible imperfection. Plastic skin and synthetic sameness are defects when natural realism is intended. |
| Illustration | Authored shape vocabulary, intentional simplification, palette/texture, dimensional consistency and brand fit; photographic anatomy/detail is not the goal. |
| Objects / product | Geometry, material response, reflections, scale, perspective, support/contact and light. Suspension must look deliberate. |
| Abstract / patterns | A legible association or compositional purpose; scale, density, repetition/variation and type relationship. Complexity and gradient shapes alone establish neither. |
| Mockups | Correct screen geometry, perspective, occlusion, reflections and scale; preserve exact product UI fidelity. Keep critical interface content native and editable when precision matters. |

Name the failed cue, then edit/regenerate with that cause corrected, choose a clearer
metaphor, or omit the asset. If reasonable attempts do not close the gap, use a strong
simpler native composition. Time spent generating is not a reason to keep weak work.
Rejection means exclusion from the design, not deletion of retained user references.

## Prepare and integrate

### Presenting accurate product UI

Product UI design and marketing presentation are different decisions. Choose the
treatment that communicates the mechanism and visual thesis: native flat surface,
layered window, floating plane, device, environmental/physical display, perspective,
overlapping product states, cropped detail or UI close-up. Neither devices nor 3D
are required; use a scene only when it materially improves the product story.

Keep the exact UI master editable in Figma. For a useful realistic device/scene,
generate or prepare the hardware, environment, lighting and atmosphere with available
image tools; inspect that asset before placement. Never generate readable product UI
to replace the accurate master. Fit the native master directly for a front-facing
screen; if actual perspective is needed, export the master and use a supported precise
compositor/renderer to map its four screen corners, crop and occlusion. Retain the
editable master and disclose that the placed perspective derivative is rasterized.
Do not assume an affine Figma transform supplies projective perspective or that an
image generator preserves text. If accurate compositing tools are unavailable, choose
a front-facing/native presentation that works or state the specific gap.

Match aspect ratio, screen perspective, scale, light direction, contact/cast shadows,
reflections and screen boundaries; preserve legibility and avoid double shadows or
UI leaking over a bezel. Inspect the composite against its UI master and then the
fresh placed Figma render. Re-export after changes to the UI; stale mockup pixels
cannot verify the new master. This conditional presentation judgment was added from
user benchmark feedback on 2026-09-05; it makes no claim of a built-in mockup engine.

Use an isolated foreground with real alpha when independent placement benefits the
composition; keep a coherent environment when its background is useful. Inspect
cutouts against the intended light/dark surroundings: halos, matte fringes, hair/fine
detail, thin edges, translucent interiors and accidental holes. A painted checkerboard
is not transparency. When file access permits, check the actual alpha channel as well
as the preview; a checkerboard shown in pixels proves nothing about transparency.
Edit masking/background/edges with available image tools;
preserve transparency and inspect the prepared file again. A mask cannot fix meaning.
Preparation may require isolation, cleanup/retouching, removal, expansion/reframing,
relighting, recoloring or grading. Change the implicated property while preserving
subject and master invariants. Generation output is a candidate, not a finished asset.

Separate environment, foreground, contact/cast shadow and graphic overlay only when
their independent positioning, lighting or responsive treatment is useful. Match
light, perspective, scale, occlusion and color with neighboring surfaces; avoid double
shadows. Keep meaningful product text, controls, data and typography native in Figma.
Generated material can supply imagery/light; it should not fabricate critical UI copy.

Inspect the **placed Figma composition** at real reading scale and every in-scope
responsive crop. Cropping, shading and overlays must not erase identifying cues,
obscure content or change the metaphor. Compare with the asset absent: keep it only
if the complete section improves. An isolated asset pass is not an integration pass;
after edits, recheck both meaning and the fresh affected render.

Technical basis verified 2026-09-05: [Figma image integration](https://developers.figma.com/docs/plugins/working-with-images/)
stores images as node fills. Use currently supported import/paint operations and
formats; preserve editable companion layers. This API capability does not certify
semantic fidelity, masking quality or visual acceptance.
