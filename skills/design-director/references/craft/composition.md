# Composition and layout craft

Use for whole-frame hierarchy, relationships, responsive organization, or a visible
composition defect. Begin with content and task structure, then choose a spatial idea.

## Establish the composition

- Identify the dominant object or decision, its supporting context, and the route the
  eye or action should take. Make that relationship apparent through placement,
  scale, sequence, contrast, and negative space.
- Choose a grid from real content: fixed work areas, fluid reading measures, aligned
  records, asymmetric editorial fields, or mixed regions. A grid coordinates; it
  does not require every object to occupy identical columns or cards.
- Organize visual mass and void across the viewport. Check silhouette at thumbnail
  scale, hierarchy at working scale, and important detail close up.
- Use alignment to reveal relationships. Break an axis only for a stronger hierarchy,
  transition, or expression that remains legible.
- Design the sequence beyond the first viewport when the artifact includes scrolling,
  overview/detail, or multiple connected screens. Vary pace without losing continuity.

## Adapt without flattening

Preserve priority, grouping, and usable order across relevant sizes rather than
shrinking a desktop arrangement. Decide what wraps, reflows, pins, scrolls, collapses,
or moves into another view. Validate extreme content and interaction targets. Use
platform conventions where they serve orientation; adaptation need not erase the
visual thesis.

## Diagnose

Distinguish structural weakness from decoration. If the wrong object dominates,
fix hierarchy. If connected content feels scattered, fix grouping or axes. If the
page feels generic, revisit the spatial thesis and reference evidence before adding
effects. If density is hard to scan, inspect information relationships before adding
empty space. Compare at equivalent content and viewport; a cropped reference cannot
prove whole-page pacing.

remain platform examples rather than mandatory values.
