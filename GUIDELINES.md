# Guidelines by work type

- Read the relevant section; combine sections for mixed products.
- Adapt priorities to the user's job. No prescribed layout or required effects.

## Landing pages and campaigns

- Communicate value, credible proof and the next action through reading and scrolling.
- Favor visible composition and purposeful scroll interactions over hidden hover effects.
- Use interactive demos when they explain value; keep essential selling points visible.
- Let useful copy and evidence earn the CTA.

## Brand websites, portfolios and service sites

- Establish credibility through the work, offering and identity.
- Show enough substance to assess fit without opening every project.
- Keep navigation and contact easy to find.
- Use imagery and transitions to strengthen identity while preserving orientation.
- Clarify who the service suits and what happens after an inquiry.

## Dashboards and analytics

- Lead with what changed, why it matters and where to investigate or act.
- Give numbers context, units and useful comparisons; choose charts for the question.
- Keep detail discoverable and filters understandable.
- Use motion for updates and continuity; keep repeated use fast.
- Give workspace to decision-relevant information, not decorative metrics.

## Working applications, admin tools and editors

- Prioritize clear controls, useful density, predictable state and fast feedback.
- Preserve selections, edits and location between tasks.
- Make frequent actions obvious; offer accessible alternatives to hover shortcuts.
- Explain save/sync status and recovery where relevant.

## Ecommerce, catalogs and marketplaces

- Help users find, compare and trust the right option.
- Make imagery, distinguishing attributes, price and availability easy to evaluate.
- Preserve browsing context across filters and product views.
- Show material costs and terms before commitment; provide clear cart feedback.
- Build conversion through confidence and reduced friction, without fake urgency.

## Onboarding, forms, checkout and account flows

- Ask for information when its purpose is clear; reuse known answers.
- Group steps to reduce effort; show progress when it helps orientation.
- Keep labels, requirements and actionable errors visible; preserve input after failure.
- Explain completion, next steps and account recovery.
- Use motion for continuity without delaying input or task completion.

## Content sites, documentation and search

- Optimize finding, reading and returning to information.
- Use descriptive headings, readable line lengths and clear link destinations.
- Provide navigation, search or in-page orientation appropriate to content volume.
- Keep essential content accessible without animation or hover.
- Place CTAs where they support the reader's next useful step.

## AI product experiences

- Explain capabilities and limits through useful examples and the actual task.
- Show progress honestly; let users inspect, edit and recover from results.
- Preserve context and provide sources when the product supports grounded answers.
- Choose chat, direct manipulation or structured controls to fit the job.
- Give users appropriate control before consequential actions.

## Features, sections and components

- Improve the local job within the surrounding product's visual and interaction language.
- Use appropriate semantics and inspect relevant states and container widths.
- Make each section add useful information, action or expression.
- Use hover to enrich feedback; keep essential meaning and actions accessible without it.

## Reusable templates and design systems

- Support realistic variation in content, density and states.
- Share tokens and mechanics that stabilize quality; keep composition flexible.
- Make editable content and intended component usage clear.
- Demonstrate useful defaults with honest sample data.
- Keep brand-specific choices explicit and abstractions proportional to actual reuse.
